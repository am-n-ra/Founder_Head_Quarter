# Apple — Case Study

## Problem
Wozniak wanted a personal computer he could afford and use. The problem was daily friction: no accessible, affordable computer existed for hobbyists.

## Sacrifice
Jobs sold his VW van ($1,000), Wozniak sold his HP-65 calculator ($500). No outside funding initially. Irreversible: they had no transportation, no tools.

## Demo
Apple I motherboard shown at Homebrew Computer Club. Imperfect (no keyboard, no case, no power supply) — but it demonstrated the concept.

## Distribution
Big-fish: first contract was a local computer shop (The Byte Shop) for 50 units at $500 each. Then expanded through retail.

## Early Believer
Mike Markkula invested $250,000 (1977). He was a retired Intel executive approached through the network.

## Incorporation
Trigger-based: incorporated January 1977 after Apple II development needed formal structure.

## Inflexion
Apple II + VisiCalc spreadsheet (1979). Business users bought Apple IIs specifically for VisiCalc. Unpredictable: nobody predicted the killer app.
