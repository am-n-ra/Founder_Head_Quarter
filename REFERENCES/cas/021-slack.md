# Slack — Growth-Stage Case Study

## Background (7-Phase Model)

Slack began as an internal tool inside Tiny Speck, a gaming company building "Glitch." The game failed in 2012. But the team's internal communication tool — built to coordinate distributed development — had become essential to how they worked. Butterfield bet the company on it.

## G1 — Seed: PMF Signals

Slack never needed to *search* for PMF the way most startups do. The product emerged from a real, lived problem the team experienced daily (distributed coordination). The PMF signals were unusually strong from the start:

- **Preview launch (Aug 2013)**: 8,000 companies requested access in 24 hours. Zero marketing. Pure word-of-mouth from the team's networks.
- **Sean Ellis score**: When Slack formally measured it before the public launch (Feb 2014), **51% of users said they'd be "very disappointed" without Slack** — well above the 40% threshold. For context, this was measured on the preview user base before any paid tier existed.
- **Organic spread**: Users didn't need to be sold — they brought the product into their own organizations. IT departments that tried to block it faced user revolt.
- **Daily engagement**: Exceptionally high DAU/MAU ratio for a B2B product. Users lived in it.

**What this means for the framework**: Slack's PMF was never in doubt after the preview launch. The Sean Ellis score confirmed what the demand signal already showed. The 40% threshold held: Slack was above it, and it scaled effortlessly.

## G2 — Series A: T2D3 Growth

Slack's ARR progression after public launch (Feb 2014) is a textbook T2D3 case:

| Year | ARR | Growth | Multiple |
|------|-----|--------|----------|
| 2014 | ~$12M | — | Launch year |
| 2015 | ~$36M | 3x | Triple |
| 2016 | ~$100M | 2.8x | Near-triple |
| 2017 | ~$200M | 2x | Double |
| 2018 | ~$400M | 2x | Double |
| 2019 | $800M+ | 2x | Double |

Slack hit $100M ARR faster than any SaaS company before it (2.5 years from launch). It went public via direct listing in 2019 at ~$800M ARR.

**The T2D3 pattern held almost perfectly** — but note the *prerequisite*: Slack had unambiguous PMF before scaling. The growth engine was amplifying demand that already existed, not creating it. This is exactly the Startup Genome finding applied positively: PMF first, then T2D3 works.

## G3 — Series B/C: Rule of 40 Transition

Post-IPO (2019), Slack's growth decelerated — not because of execution failure, but because the collaboration market matured and Microsoft Teams entered aggressively. By FY2021 (~$900M ARR), growth had slowed to ~30-40%.

**Then Salesforce acquired Slack for $27.7B (Dec 2020)** — an exit that valued Slack at roughly 30x forward ARR, reflecting its strategic position rather than near-term growth rate. Post-acquisition, Slack's Rule of 40 profile improved as it focused on profitability within Salesforce's portfolio.

## What This Case Verifies

1. **Sean Ellis 40% threshold**: Slack scored 51% and scaled effortlessly. Confirms the benchmark.
2. **T2D3 pattern**: Held perfectly, but *only because PMF was already proven*. T2D3 amplified existing demand, didn't create it.
3. **Premature scaling risk (avoided)**: Slack didn't spend on growth until PMF was confirmed. Its growth spend followed the demand curve, not preceded it.
4. **Limitation**: Slack is a near-best-case scenario — the product emerged from a lived problem with an existing user base. Not all G1 journeys look this clean.
