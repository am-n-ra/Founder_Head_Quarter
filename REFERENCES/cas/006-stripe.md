# Stripe — Case Study

## Problem
Patrick Collison experienced the pain of integrating payments while building other products. Each payment processor required weeks of paperwork, PCI compliance, bank accounts. The problem was personal: he hated dealing with payments.

## Sacrifice
Both brothers dropped out of elite universities (MIT and Harvard). Irreversible academic sacrifice.

## Demo
In 2008, during their YC batch, they built a tool for themselves to accept payments. Stripe started as an internal tool — 7 lines of code. Product was initially imperfect (limited features, US-only, no fraud protection).

## Distribution
Niche: started with developers, then startups, then grew to platforms and enterprise. The API was the product was the distribution.

## Early Believer
YC (Paul Graham) initially, then the developer community. Stripe's positioning was "developer-first payments" — a radical departure from legacy processors.

## Incorporation
Day-one: incorporated immediately in 2009 before building the product (pattern rare — usually trigger-based).

## Inflexion
Developer community adoption (2011). Stripe's API became the standard for startups. Unpredictable: nobody predicted that developer experience would win the payments market.
