# Zoom — Growth-Stage Case Study

## Why This Case Matters

Zoom is the rare company that provides clean data across ALL growth stages: G1 (PMF via NRR retention), G2 (T2D3 before COVID), G3 (Rule of 40 as growth normalized), and G4 (governance delegation). It also illustrates the unpredictability of inflexion (COVID) better than any other case in this library.

## G1 — Seed: PMF by Retention

Zoom didn't formally run a Sean Ellis test — it launched before the framework was widely adopted (2012-2013). But the PMF signals are unmistakable:

- **Net Revenue Retention >130%** in the first 3 years. Users didn't just stay — they expanded. Teams that tried Zoom with one meeting room bought licenses for the whole company.
- **Zero-touch adoption**: Individual users brought Zoom into organizations without a sales process. The product spread virally within companies.
- **Competitive displacement**: Users chose Zoom over WebEx, GoToMeeting, and Skype despite those being entrenched — because the experience was dramatically better.

**Retention as PMF proxy**: Zoom validates the Section 22 claim that for non-survey-situations, NRR >120% is a reliable PMF signal. When existing customers expand faster than they churn, the product has crossed the threshold.

## G2 — Series A/B: T2D3 (Before COVID)

Zoom's pre-COVID growth from 2015 to 2019 follows T2D3 precisely — $10M → $30M → $100M → ~$300M → ~$600M ARR. This was organic: the product was better, it spread by itself, and the company invested in enterprise sales *after* the PMF signal was clear.

**Crucial detail**: Zoom raised only $42.5M in venture capital ($6M + $6.5M + $30M) before its $100M Series D. That's capital-efficient T2D3 — the growth was real before the big checks arrived. The $100M Series D (at $1B valuation) was a *consequence* of the pattern, not a cause.

## G3 — Series C/D: Rule of 40 at Scale

Zoom's post-IPO trajectory (2021-2024) illustrates G3 dynamics:

| Metric | 2021 (Peak) | 2024 (Normalized) |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| ARR | ~$4.5B | ~$4.7B |
| Growth | ~50% | ~3.6% |
| FCF Margin | ~30% | >40% |
| Rule of 40 | ~80 | ~44 |

At $4.7B ARR with 3.6% growth and 40%+ FCF margins, Zoom's Rule of 40 score is ~44 — comfortably above 40. But it achieves this through profitability, not growth. This is the classic G3 trade-off: as the market matures, efficiency replaces growth as the primary metric.

**Critical nuance**: Zoom's NRR from enterprise dropped to 98% (2024) — below 100% for the first time. This is the G3/G4 risk: even with strong Rule of 40, if the core product's expansion revenue can't offset churn, long-term value erodes. Section 22's honesty about Rule of 40 being "for mature companies" is exactly right — it tells you about efficiency, not about whether the product has a growth future.

## G4 — Governance / Late-Stage

Post-COVID, Zoom shifted to a functional-lead model. Eric Yuan personally took over the Contact Center product line, while the day-to-day operating model moved to functional executives. Board independence increased as Zoom's investor base matured.

## What This Case Verifies

1. **T2D3**: Confirmed for a capital-efficient SaaS company. But note Zoom raised conservatively — it proved the pattern before raising large rounds, not the reverse.
2. **Rule of 40**: Confirmed at G3 maturity (44 score). But Zoom's low post-COVID growth also shows the limit of the Rule — it's a snapshot of efficiency, not a measure of long-term product health.
3. **Inflection unpredictability**: COVID was the most unplannable inflection in modern business history. Zoom happened to be positioned for it (high-quality video, reliable infrastructure). But no one could have predicted it.
4. **Premature scaling (avoided)**: Zoom scaled its sales org after PMF and organic growth were proven, not before.
