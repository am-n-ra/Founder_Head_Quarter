# DeepSeek — Case Study

## Problem
Liang Wenfeng (founder of High-Flyer, a quant hedge fund) believed AI was the next technological frontier. The problem: Chinese AI labs were dependent on US chips and lagging behind OpenAI. The problem was personal: as a quant fund manager, he saw the limits of existing AI models daily.

## Sacrifice
Redirected profits from a successful high-frequency trading firm into AI research. Personal reputation on the line — if the AI bet failed, it would affect the core business.

## Demo
DeepSeek Coder (2023), DeepSeek V2 (2024), DeepSeek-R1 (Jan 2025). Each showed Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture beating models 10x the size. Product was initially imperfect — not as polished as GPT-4 on some tasks.

## Distribution
Open: released models open-source with MIT license. Gained distribution through HuggingFace, developer communities, and GitHub. The openness was deliberate counter-positioning against closed labs.

## Early Believer
Chinese AI researchers and the open-source ML community. The positioning was "GPT-4 at 1/10 the cost, open-source."

## Incorporation
Trigger-based: created as a project under High-Flyer, later spun off.

## Inflexion
DeepSeek-R1 release (January 2025). Caused a $1T drop in US tech stocks. Completely unpredictable: nobody expected a Chinese quant fund to produce frontier AI.
