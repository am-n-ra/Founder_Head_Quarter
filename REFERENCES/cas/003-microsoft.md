# Microsoft — Case Study

## Problem
Bill Gates and Paul Allen read about the Altair 8800 in Popular Electronics. They realized microcomputers needed software. The problem: no BASIC interpreter existed for the new platform.

## Sacrifice
Gates dropped out of Harvard. Definitively irreversible — Harvard was a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity.

## Demo
They called MITS claiming to have a BASIC interpreter — they didn't yet. Wrote it in 4 weeks without ever having seen an Altair. Demo worked on first try (1975). Product was imperfect (ran in 4KB memory, limited features) — but it worked.

## Distribution
Big-fish: licensed BASIC to MITS, then to every other computer manufacturer (Apple, Commodore, Tandy, IBM).

## Early Believer
MITS (Ed Roberts) — signed a licensing deal after the demo. Not an investor — a customer who believed.

## Incorporation
Trigger-based: incorporated 1976 after MITS deal was signed, when they needed legal structure for licensing.

## Inflexion
IBM PC contract (1980). Microsoft didn't own the OS at first — they bought QDOS for $50K and adapted it. Unpredictable: IBM chose Microsoft over Digital Research because of a non-disclosure dispute.
